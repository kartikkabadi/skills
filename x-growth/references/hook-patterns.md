# Hook patterns for X

Expanded reference for Rule 2 of the skill. Each pattern: the formula, why it
works, and a bland → spicy rewrite. The starred examples are the ones that
actually performed in the source video.

## 1. Kill the incumbent

- Formula: "It's time to cancel your \<tool\> subscription." / "Stop paying
  for \<category\>."
- Why: names a concrete product people feel locked into and implies the
  replacement is in the post. Specific + a little outrageous = replies.
- Real: "It's time to cancel your Opus Clip subscription" — the author's most
  viral post.
- Rewrite: "I built a video clipping script" → "Your $30/mo clipping tool is
  now a 40-line script. Here it is."

## 2. Deadline + holdout jab

- Formula: "In \<timeframe\>, only \<unflattering group\> will still be
  \<doing it the old way\>."
- Why: the timeframe makes the claim falsifiable; the jab gives holdouts a
  reason to argue in the replies.
- Real: "In six months, only insane people are going to be editing their
  videos manually" — 160K views.
- Rewrite: "AI note-taking is getting good" → "By next year, only masochists
  will still write meeting notes by hand."

## 3. Gatekeep inversion

- Formula: take the exact thing the user almost did not share and lead with
  it. "This is the \<tool/workflow/prompt\> I use every day. Here it is."
- Why: the hoarding instinct is a quality signal — the tools built for
  personal use are the smartest and rarest. Giving them away in full is the
  entire point (Rule 1).
- Rewrite: "I have a system for writing threads" → "This prompt writes my
  threads. I almost kept it to myself — here it is."

## 4. Underdog build

- Formula: "I'm not a \<credential\>. I built \<real artifact\> anyway." /
  "Non-technical, and I just shipped \<thing\>."
- Why: X rewards shipped artifacts over titles; the missing credential is the
  hook, the demo is the proof.
- Rewrite: "Learning to code with AI" → "I can't code. I shipped a working app
  this weekend anyway — here's the video."

## 5. Numbers with a tight timeframe

- Formula: "\<specific result\> in \<surprisingly short time\>."
- Why: concrete numbers are checkable and compress a story into one line.
- Rewrite: "Posting on X is working for me" → "0 to 3,000 followers in a few
  weeks, as a non-technical account. The playbook:"

## The reply-gate close (not a hook — the ending)

- Formula: "I put the full \<guide/template/prompt pack\> together — comment
  and I'll send it."
- Why: turns reach into replies and DMs; replies feed the algorithm; the DM
  thread starts a relationship.
- Rule: the asset must exist and be sent. Never bait a reply-gate with
  nothing behind it.

## What "spicy" is not

- Not vague hype: "This changes everything", "You won't believe this."
- Not a false claim: every hook claim must be backed in the body or a reply.
- Not borrowed outrage: the jab lands on a practice ("editing manually"),
  not on a person or a group identity.
- Not buried: if the hook needs a second sentence to make sense, it is not
  the hook.
