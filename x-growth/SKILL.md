# X Growth

Playbook distilled from a creator who grew 0 → ~3,000 followers in a few weeks
as a non-technical builder on X. Use it to draft posts, plan a content
strategy, or critique drafts that are not landing.

## Rule 1: Never gatekeep

The instinct "this idea/tool/workflow is too good to share" marks exactly what
performs best. The tools and workflows the user built for themselves are the
strongest content they have — concrete, tested, and rare. Share them in full:
the setup, the prompts, the config, the results.

When drafting: if the post would make a reader ask "ok, but how?", it is
gatekeeping. Put the how in the post or an attached artifact (screenshot,
gist, template). A post that withholds the goods reads as a tease and
underperforms.

## Rule 2: Hooks carry the post

The first line decides the reach. The spicier the hook, the better — but
spicy means a concrete, arguable claim, not vague hype ("this will change
everything") or a lie.

Proven patterns (full table and rewrites in
[references/hook-patterns.md](references/hook-patterns.md)):

- **Kill the incumbent** — "It's time to cancel your \<tool\> subscription."
  Names a real product people pay for; implies the replacement is in the post.
- **Deadline + holdout jab** — "In six months, only insane people will still
  be \<doing X the old way\>." The timeframe makes it falsifiable; the jab
  forces replies.
- **Gatekeep inversion** — lead with the thing the user was tempted to hoard.
- **Underdog build** — "I'm not a developer. I shipped \<real thing\> anyway."
  X rewards shipped artifacts over credentials.

Hook checklist: stands alone as line one • names a specific tool, number, or
timeframe • a skeptic would want to reply • the body can actually back it.

## Rule 3: Build in public, with AI

X values builders and people who ship products — unlike LinkedIn, credentials
do not move it. Posting the thing the user made — rough, small, weird — beats
posting about what they know. Every build is multiple posts: the demo, the
failure, the fix, the numbers.

For a non-technical account this is the wedge: AI tools let the user ship real
artifacts, and "non-technical person shipped X" is itself a hook. If the user
has been hesitant to share what they made with AI, push them to post it.

## The engagement mechanic: reply-gated assets

When the advice is longer than a post, split it: hook post + the extended
asset (guide, template, prompt pack) offered in the replies — "I put the full
guide together; comment and I'll send it." This converts reach into replies
and DMs, and replies feed the algorithm. The asset must actually exist and be
sent — a broken promise burns the audience.

## Drafting checklist

- Line one is a standalone hook: no "I", no throat-clearing, no context-first.
- The post gives away the real thing — never "DM me for the actual answer" on
  the core claim.
- A build, demo, or asset is shown, not described.
- One idea per post; the rest becomes the thread or the reply-gated guide.
- Every claim in the hook is backed in the body or a follow-up reply.

## Guardrails

- Never post, reply, quote, or DM on X without explicit user approval. Produce
  drafts and plans only.
- Never fabricate metrics, followers, or results in a draft. Use the user's
  real numbers or a placeholder they fill.
- Spicy is a claim, not a tone: keep the user's voice, avoid engagement-bait
  phrasing that does not match how they write.
